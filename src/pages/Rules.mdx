## One-Minute Overview

| What's the Same | What's Different |
|-----------------|------------------|
| 8×8 board, 16 pieces per side | Back-rank pieces have **limited moves** |
| King & Pawns move normally | 7 **Anomalies** replace Queen/Rooks/Bishops/Knights |
| Checkmate wins | Each Anomaly has **10 charges** that deplete |
| Captures work identically | Pieces can become **Gridlocked** (frozen) |
| | **No castling — [Override](#override-when-the-king-takes-the-wheel) instead** |

> **What's an Anomaly?** Your 7 back-rank pieces. Unlike a normal chess piece, an Anomaly has **no fixed move of its own** — it moves *only* as its **charges** allow. All 7 are the same kind of piece; they differ *only* in how those charges are split across Leap, Orthogonal, and Diagonal moves (you'll see [exactly how](#the-core-mechanic-vector-charges) just below).

## The Constants: King & Pawns

Start with what you already know. **King and Pawns move normally** — unlike the 7 Anomalies, they have no charges to spend, so they never run dry:

| Piece | Rules |
|-------|-------|
| **King** | 1 square any direction. Must escape check. **No castling.** |
| **Pawns** | Standard chess pawns — 2-square first move, en passant, and captures diagonally. |

### Gridlock: When a Piece Runs Dry

When an Anomaly hits **zero across all three vectors**, it's **Gridlocked**—frozen in place for good:

| Gridlocked pieces… | …but they still… |
|--------------------|------------------|
| ❌ Can't move | ✅ Block paths |
| ❌ Can't threaten | ✅ Get captured |

> ⚠️ **Key Insight:** A Gridlocked piece goes gray and becomes dead weight. Use yours as shields—or bleed enemy charges dry and let their frozen pieces wall them in.

### Random Openings

Every game starts fresh — no opening book to memorize.

**What's randomized:**
- **Position** — King and 7 Anomalies shuffle across the back rank
- **Composition** — each Anomaly's archetype is rolled at random
- **Balance** — charges always split 24 / 23 / 23 across the three vectors

Both sides get the **same mirrored army** — fair variety, never a coin-flip advantage.

> 🌐 Online PvP deals the same Balanced armies to both players.

### How Many Openings? (≈5.95 Billion)

**The short version:** just over **5.95 billion** different boards the game *can* deal (exactly 5,950,517,760). You'd have to play about **91,000 games** before you'd even have a coin-flip chance of seeing the same board twice — in other words, **you will never replay the same opening.**

**Four simple guardrails keep every Balanced army fair:**

1. **Exact totals** — the seven Anomalies' charges always add up to **24 / 23 / 23** across Leap / Orthogonal / Diagonal. Same budget, every game.
2. **At most 2 Absolutes** — no more than two "one-trick" pieces that dump all 10 charges into a single direction, so the board never collapses into one kind of move.
3. **At most 2 of the same archetype** — you'll never be handed a monotonous 7-of-a-kind army.
4. **Split bishops** — if an army happens to roll two pure-Diagonal Absolutes (the only truly color-locked piece), they're placed on opposite-colored squares — one light, one dark — so you always get a genuine light-and-dark bishop pair instead of two that cover the same color.

The game builds every army to already satisfy all four — there's no lucky re-roll and no lopsided hand to fix. The final count comes from four steps:

1. **Start with every possible army.** Each Anomaly can roll 1 of 36 builds, and there are 7 of them: 36⁷ = **78,364,164,096** raw armies.
2. **Keep only the fair ones (rules 1–3).** Filtering to exactly 24 / 23 / 23 with ≤2 Absolutes and ≤2 duplicates leaves **743,855,490** valid armies.
3. **Place the King (8 files).** Each army can start with the King on any of 8 files: 743,855,490 × 8 = **5,950,843,920** boards.
4. **Split the bishops (rule 4).** Removing the rare same-color double-bishop boards drops 326,160, giving **5,950,517,760** distinct openings.

Because the sampler is **uniform**, that 5.95 billion is the *effective* variety — not just a theoretical ceiling. Every valid board is equally likely to be dealt.

## Override: When the King Takes the Wheel

A desperate, one-way gamble — board a friendly Anomaly and pilot it yourself:

| What Happens | The Catch |
|--------------|-----------|
| King steps onto a friendly Anomaly | Fuse into one **royal** piece — no dismount, ever |
| Piloted piece moves like its host | Burns 1 charge per move |
| Still counts as your King | Can be checked — and **must** respond |
| Its charges are now your life | Run them dry (0/0/0) → **Gridlocked Death**, instant loss |

<OverrideDemo />

### Gridlocked Death

If your Piloted Anomaly hits **zero across all vectors**, your King is sealed inside a **Gridlocked bunker** — an immobile royal piece with no legal moves. **Instant loss.**

> ⚠️ **The {Omni.alias} Exception:** You **cannot** Override a {Omni.alias} — the pawn that promoted into it is already piloting.

## How to Win

| Outcome | Trigger |
|---------|---------|
| **Win** | Checkmate the enemy King, or the opponent resigns |
| **Win** | Opponent's Piloted Anomaly suffers **Gridlocked Death** (runs dry while carrying the King) |
| **Draw** | Stalemate, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, or all Anomalies Gridlocked with no pawn moves left |

## Strategy Tips

1. **Spend every charge effectively** — each charge is burned permanently and can never be recharged, so make every move count instead of wasting one just to reposition.
2. **Track the vector math** — each piece's Leap, Orthogonal, and Diagonal charges deplete independently, and its *movement shape* narrows as they run dry. Watch this on both sides — read what your key pieces (and your opponent's) can *still* do and plan a move ahead, so a shape change never catches you off guard.
3. **Bleed them dry** — pressure an enemy Anomaly so it must keep moving to stay safe; every escape spends a charge. A piece you push to **Gridlock** freezes into a dead wall you can box them in with.
4. **Spread the load** — moving one piece over and over drains it into Gridlock while the rest sit idle. Rotate your active pieces to keep your whole army mobile.
5. **Push pawns for {Omni.alias}s** — promotion mints a {Omni.alias} with **{Omni.sharedPool} fully-flexible charges** spendable on any vector. Fewer charges than an Anomaly's 10, but total freedom — and it's the only way to make one, so a late-game passed pawn is a real threat.
6. **Override only when cornered** — with no castling, boarding an adjacent friendly Anomaly is your King's one escape hatch: a trapped King can borrow that piece's movement to break out, or weaponize a strong host to attack. But it's a one-way commitment — **no dismount** — and every move now **burns the host's charges**. Run that pool dry and it's **Gridlocked Death — an instant loss**. Treat it as a last resort, and never board a piece that's nearly empty.

> 💡 Gridlocked pieces still block lines — use them as walls. Pawns can never be Gridlocked.
